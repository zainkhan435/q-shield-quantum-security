2. Quantum Digital Signatures (QDS)

Unlike Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), which secures peer-to-peer confidentiality between two parties, Quantum Digital Signatures (QDS) guarantee three critical cryptographic properties:

  • Authenticity: Confirms that the message was signed by Alice.
  • Integrity: Ensures no intermediary altered the payload or quantum states.
  • Non-Repudiation: Prevents Alice from denying her signature to verifiers Bob and Charlie.

3. Qubits & Two-Dimensional Hilbert Space

The fundamental quantum information carrier is the qubit in $\mathbb{C}^2$, spanning orthonormal computational basis states $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$:

|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle = \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)|0\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)|1\rangle

Where $|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1$, and $(\theta, \phi)$ correspond to spherical coordinates on the Bloch Sphere.

4. Pauli Operators & Measurement Bases

QDS signatures leverage mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) defined by the Pauli group $\mathcal{P}_1 = \{I, X, Y, Z\}$:

\sigma_x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \sigma_y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \sigma_z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}

The three measurement bases are:

  • Z-Basis: $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$ (Computational basis)
  • X-Basis: $\{|+\rangle, |-\rangle\}$ where $|\pm\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle \pm |1\rangle)$
  • Y-Basis: $\{|+i\rangle, |-i\rangle\}$ where $|\pm i\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle \pm i|1\rangle)$

6. Bennett Quantum Teleportation Protocol

Alice transmits her unknown quantum signature state $|\psi\rangle$ on $q_0$ without physical particle transfer:

  1. Joint 3-Qubit State: $|\Psi_{012}\rangle = |\psi\rangle \otimes |\Phi^+\rangle_{12}$
  2. Bell Measurement: Alice applies $\text{CNOT}(q_0, q_1)$ and $H(q_0)$, measuring in computational basis $\to (c_0, c_1) \in \{00, 01, 10, 11\}$.
  3. Classical Channel: Alice sends 2 classical bits $(c_0, c_1)$ to Bob.
  4. Bob Unitary Recovery: Bob executes $U = X^{c_1} Z^{c_0}$ on $q_2$, reconstituting state $|\psi\rangle$.

7. Projective Verification Measurements

Upon Pauli reconstruction, Bob executes projective measurement in Alice's authorized secret key basis $\mathcal{B} \in \{Z, X, Y\}$:

\Pi_0 = |\psi_{\text{target}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\text{target}}|, \quad \Pi_1 = I - \Pi_0

For legitimate signatures, $P(\text{Outcome } 0) = \text{Tr}(\Pi_0 \rho) \to 1.0$. Any orthogonal count indicates an error or adversary intervention.

9. Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) Formulation

The observed Quantum Bit Error Rate compares erroneous measurements against total shots $N$:

\text{QBER} = \frac{N_{\text{error}}}{N_{\text{total}}} = \frac{N_1}{N_0 + N_1}

In QDS protocols, the theoretical cutoff threshold is calibrated to $\text{QBER}_{\text{cutoff}} = 11.0\%$. QBER values exceeding this bound indicate eavesdropping or forgery.

10. Binomial Hypothesis Testing ($Z$-Score)

To differentiate natural environmental noise fluctuations from active cyber attacks, Q-SHIELD calculates the binomial standard deviation $Z$-score:

Z = \frac{\text{QBER}_{\text{observed}} - p_{\text{expected}}}{\sqrt{\frac{p_{\text{expected}}(1 - p_{\text{expected}})}{N}}}

If $Z \ge 3.0\sigma$, the probability that the error spike is random noise is $p < 0.0027$ (99.73% confidence), triggering a deterministic intrusion alarm.
